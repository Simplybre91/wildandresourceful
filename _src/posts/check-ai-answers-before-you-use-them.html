I use AI tools every day for work, school and the house. They save me hours. They also hand me wrong answers with total confidence, and the wrong ones look exactly like the right ones.

So before I use an AI answer for anything that matters, I run a quick check. It takes about two minutes.

Check 1: Is this something that changes?

Prices, laws, phone numbers, store hours, who runs a company, what a product costs: these change all the time, and AI tools are often working from old information. If the answer depends on what's true today, look it up on the official site before you trust it.

Check 2: Ask where it came from

Type: "What is your source for that?" A solid answer points to something you can open and read. A shaky answer gets vague or changes the story. If you can't find the source yourself, treat the answer as a guess.

Check 3: Check the numbers by hand

AI tools still slip on math, especially with percentages, totals and dates. If the answer has a number you're going to use, a price, a dose, a measurement, a deadline, redo the math on your phone calculator.

Real example

I asked for a materials total on a small job. The list was right. The total was off because one line got counted twice. Thirty seconds with a calculator caught it.

Check 4: Ask a second way

Ask the same question with different words, or ask a different AI tool. If the two answers disagree, that's your sign to slow down and check a real source.

When to skip the AI completely

For medical, legal and money decisions, use the AI to help you understand the topic and write your questions. Then take those questions to an actual doctor, lawyer or professional.
